Ideation: Google Earth Route World

This is a ranked ideation pass for making goDiesel feel like realistic map navigation with a game-like route layer, without bouncing the user between Street View, personal photos, satellite panels, and flat map views.

Date: 2026-06-13 Topic: google-earth-route-world Mode: repo-grounded

Codebase Context

The current app has already moved away from static personal photos toward a route playback experience: MapLibre powers the main atlas, Google Street View powers the route-cam, and Three.js powers route cinema experiments. The spike at docs/spikes/2026-06-13-google-earth-route-navigation.md shows that the local Google key can fetch the Photorealistic 3D Tiles root tileset, so a real Earth-style prototype is feasible rather than a fake terrain-map approximation.

Continuity gap Street View is vivid but sparse. It works best as a ground-level moment, not as the primary world.
Trust gap Ungeotagged or mismatched personal photos undermine the promise that the route world is real.
Technical opening Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles plus CesiumJS can give the route one continuous, photorealistic base.

Topic Axes

Primary world modelWhich visual source owns the route experience.
Camera and motionHow the app makes movement feel authored, smooth, and legible.
Ground-level realismHow Street View fits without creating jarring source switches.
Quest/game layerHow the route becomes playable while staying truthful to place.
Coverage and fallbackHow the app handles sparse imagery, weak devices, cost, and mobile constraints.

Ranked Ideas

1. Earth Replay Lab 2. Route Director 3. Windshield Moments 4. Quest Signal Layer 5. Coverage Preflight 6. Memory Trailer Export
1

Earth Replay Lab

Conf 90%HighPrimary world
DescriptionBuild ?lab=earth as a desktop-first route viewer using CesiumJS and Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles. The route line, marker, playback progress, and camera all live in one 3D world. MapLibre remains the stable atlas outside the lab.
Basisdirect: the spike states the local key returned the Google 3D Tiles root tileset and recommends CesiumJS because the goal is a continuous cinematic 3D route world, not a pitched map.
RationaleThis directly addresses the user's core ask: realistic map/navigation with a game-like route layer, one seamless visual source, and no fake terrain cam.
DownsidesHigh runtime weight, GPU cost, billing exposure, and camera tuning risk. Needs isolation until the experience is proven.
ComplexityHigh
2

Route Director

Conf 84%MediumCamera
DescriptionAdd a route camera director with named motion states: scout ahead, chase, climb reveal, summit orbit, city glide, and finish pullback. It uses route bearing, slope, curvature, and progress milestones to choose motion language.
Basisdirect: the app already smooths bearings and drives route playback; the spike calls out camera height, lookahead, pitch, and route progress sync as core prototype work.
RationalePhotorealistic tiles alone will not make the experience shine. The product feeling comes from controlled motion that understands when to look forward, pull back, slow down, or reveal terrain.
DownsidesBad defaults can cause nausea, clipping, or confusion. Needs a small set of conservative states before more dramatic choreography.
ComplexityMedium
3

Windshield Moments

Conf 82%MediumGround realism
DescriptionKeep Street View as a deliberate "windshield" moment that appears only at high-confidence route segments or checkpoints. The primary Earth world never splits into a second competing panel by default; instead, Street View appears as a short portal, pause-state, or optional inset with a clear label.
Basisdirect: the dogfood report says Street View was materially closer to the requested experience than map cam, but also records sparse and discontinuous coverage.
RationaleThis preserves the best part of deployed Street View without letting coverage gaps define the whole UX. It also matches the user's desire for a single coherent source, because the route world remains Earth-first.
DownsidesRequires precomputing or caching coverage and being strict about when the portal is allowed to appear.
ComplexityMedium
4

Quest Signal Layer

Conf 78%MediumQuest layer
DescriptionOverlay a restrained game layer on the Earth world: glowing route ribbon, objective gates, climb beats, checkpoint labels, finish burst, and a minimal HUD. It should feel like a real route with meaning added, not a fantasy world replacing the place.
Basisreasoned: the product thesis is "real routes, playable days." The route must become game-like through interaction, progression, and feedback while the underlying world stays truthful.
RationaleThis is where goDiesel becomes distinct from Google Earth, Strava, or a maps demo. It converts a real run into a quest preview.
DownsidesEasy to overdecorate. The layer must stay sparse enough that the photorealistic world remains the hero.
ComplexityMedium
5

Coverage Preflight

Conf 76%LowFallbacks
DescriptionBefore playback, score each route for Earth tile availability, Street View coverage, approximate device capability, route length, and expected load cost. Show a quiet readiness state and pick a deliberate experience: Earth replay, Earth with windshield moments, atlas-only, or desktop-only.
Basisdirect: the dogfood report identifies sparse Street View and external resource loading as UX risks; the spike calls out coverage, cost, performance, and mobile as explicit risks.
RationaleThe app should never surprise the user with a blank panel, fake cam, or broken photorealistic promise. Preflight lets the product be ambitious and honest.
DownsidesAdds precomputation logic and may initially produce conservative results until thresholds are tuned.
ComplexityLow
6

Memory Trailer Export

Conf 66%HighShare
DescriptionAfter Earth replay works interactively, add a short route trailer mode: 12-20 seconds, route intro, climb reveal, one windshield moment when available, finish title card, and shareable output. This replaces static share cards as the premium artifact.
Basisexternal: cinematic route previews are a familiar product pattern in fitness and travel apps; reasoned: goDiesel's value is emotional route memory, and motion sells that better than a still card.
RationaleThis gives the experience a distribution loop without compromising the core route viewer. It should come after the Earth viewer, not before it.
DownsidesExporting video or deterministic captures is a separate technical project and can distract from fixing live playback first.
ComplexityHigh

Rejection Summary

Full Google Earth cloneToo broad and not required to validate route playback.
Replace MapLibre immediatelyDestabilizes the working atlas before the Earth mode proves itself.
AI-generated route worldsToo likely to feel fake, expensive, and disconnected from the user's real route.
Street View primary rendererGround imagery is too discontinuous for trails, mountains, and sparse coverage areas.
Default personal-photo layerPhotos are not reliably geolocated and already conflict with the desired route truth.
Map-cam fallback as "terrain cam"The user has already rejected zoomed-in map views as not photorealistic enough.
